## Estimates

Regarding the **estimates** that you did for practical tasks...

### How was your estimate accuracy usually?

At first, my estimates were quite inaccurate. I often thought tasks would be quicker to finish than they actually were.
I didn’t fully consider time spent debugging or understanding new concepts, so my actual work time was usually longer
than expected.

### How did your estimate accuracy improve or change during the course of the subject?

Over time, my estimation skills improved because I started to base my guesses on past practicals. By prac_09, my time
predictions became more realistic since I had a better idea of how much effort coding, testing, and refining programs
usually required. Still, I sometimes underestimated when new or unfamiliar topics came up.

### What did you learn from doing these estimates?

I learned that making accurate estimates comes from experience, not luck. It taught me to track my progress and reflect
on how I spend time. This made me more aware of how I plan my work and helped me manage my time better in later
practicals.

## Code Reviews

### What have you learned from being reviewed by other people?

Getting feedback from others helped me notice details I often overlook, such as naming conventions, indentation, and
consistent commenting. It also gave me new ideas about how to structure functions more clearly. Seeing how others
interpret my code helped me understand how important readability is when someone else has to review or use it.

### What have you learned from doing code reviews of other people?

Reviewing other people’s code made me more critical and aware of good programming habits. It was easier to identify
unclear logic or repetition in their code, which made me more careful when writing my own. I also learned how different
approaches can solve the same problem, which helped me think more creatively and improve my coding style.
